# Atlantis content guide

## Voice

Write in plain, direct language. Be calm, specific, and informative. Welcome visitors by answering practical questions, not through sentimental slogans or promotional claims.

Avoid vague recovery language, quasi-spiritual filler, medical claims, promises of outcomes, and statements that assume every Recovery Dharma meeting follows one format. Name Buddhist concepts plainly when they matter; do not use them as decoration or turn the site into an academic explanation.

## Ownership

- Public facts and wording live in `src/content/`.
- Local community facts must come from an owner-approved source.
- Factual edits should not alter layout components, style files, or meeting-domain code.
- Public copy must not invent local practices, contacts, addresses, access details, affiliations, or policies.
- General Recovery Dharma facts and links must come from Recovery Dharma Global’s public website and be paraphrased in original wording.
- Medical claims and guaranteed recovery outcomes are prohibited.

## Public pages

Lead with what a visitor needs to know: what the community is, when meetings occur, what a meeting may include, and what action is available. Prefer short paragraphs and factual headings. Remove repetition rather than finding a new way to say the same thing.

The words “fictional example” normally belong in the identity badge, footer, Meetings qualification, and Connect state—not throughout every paragraph.

Before changing public copy, read `DESIGN_GUIDE.md`. Content changes must preserve route composition, meeting validation, announcement timing, Global-directory sanitization, cache, timeout, attribution, and fallback behavior.
